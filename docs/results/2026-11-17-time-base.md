# Time base in the sim: pose stamps against `/clock` (FND-03)

Measured on 17 Nov 2026 on a club laptop, on `platform/build-2027` at `dfa59e7` plus the FND-03 change. Sim image `argus-sim:humble`: ArduCopter 4.6.3 SITL, Gazebo Harmonic, MAVROS 2.16.0.

**Result: not yet a reliable pass.** Three delivery flights on sim time all landed within 0.09 m of the target with every ROS node on sim time and nearly every pose stamp time-synchronised, but the stamps agreed with the sim clock to 16.0, 23.0 and 23.9 ms at the 95th percentile against a limit of 20 ms. One flight counted and two were INVALID. The median is 10 to 13 ms. Left for PLT-06, which owns the MAVROS link and message rates.

`timesync_rate` was 10.0 as the running `/mavros/time` node reports it, the largest step of MAVROS's offset estimate was 0.06 ms, and there was no `time base UNHEALTHY` line (the guard flag is off in this scenario; see section 3). `/launch_ros_*`, `/mavros/param` and tf2's listener helper answer no parameter request and are not among the 65.

How to read the numbers:
- The difference is taken when the checker receives the pose, so it holds the stamp's own error plus the link from SITL through MAVProxy to MAVROS and on to the checker.
- Between flights 2 and 3 the checker stopped reading `/clock` in Python (250 callbacks a second ahead of every pose) and reads Gazebo's clock directly. The figure did not improve, so the checker was not the cause. Against Gazebo's clock the median also includes the delay with which MAVROS's own copy of the clock follows Gazebo's, which makes every stamp that much early.
- Flight 2 was also the first on the final detector code (the clock read moved back to the publish).
- Where the 10 to 13 ms comes from has not been pinned down. On the ground, with no mission running and the full-rate clock, the same comparison gave a median of 4.0 ms and a 95th percentile of 5.3 ms (section 2). Suspects, in order: MAVProxy (a Python process) relaying every message between SITL and MAVROS while the mission, detector and logger load the machine; MAVROS's sixty nodes each handling the clock; the 4 ms clock steps.
- What PLT-06 should try first: connect MAVROS straight to SITL's second serial port (TCP 5762) in the sim-time branch of `sim/container/start.sh`, which takes MAVProxy out of the path and stops it overriding the stream rates; then halve `STEP_NS` in `sim/clock_bridge.py`.

## What it took to get there

**1. MAVROS applies its offset late, and later still on sim time.** MAVROS 2.16 stamps a pose `time_boot_ms + offset` only once its filter has taken 500 samples (50 s at 10 Hz). Until then the stamp is the ROS clock on arrival, which agrees with `/clock` trivially. On sim time the first two windows are thrown away: SITL's clock starts equal to Gazebo's, the offset starts near zero, and MAVROS computes it in unsigned arithmetic, so a slightly negative sample becomes about 9.2e18 ns. The estimate ran off (`estimated_offset_ns: -269282761076432`, falling 8.5e13 per sample) until MAVROS itself noticed:

```
[mavros.time]: TM: Time jump detected. Resetting time synchroniser.     (58.7 s after the plugin started)
[mavros.time]: TM: Time jump detected. Resetting time synchroniser.     (110.0 s)
```

After the second reset the estimate followed the observed offset to within 0.7 ms (median, 690 samples). It recovers by itself because SITL's clock falls steadily behind Gazebo's, 0.14 to 0.29 ms per second measured, so the offset becomes safely positive after about half a minute. The start script therefore waits for synchronised stamps before READY (`time sync applied after 127s`), and the check requires at least 100 synchronised stamps so it cannot pass on arrival times.

**2. The full-rate clock was too expensive.** The first version bridged Gazebo's clock with `ros_gz_bridge`, which passes on every message (724 Hz measured). In flight:

```
    PID   %CPU  COMMAND
    417  280.0  mavros_node
    737   66.7  mission_executo
    733   53.3  simple_takeoff_
    735   46.7  target_cv
load average: 30.06        real-time factor 0.53 to 0.60 while armed
```

Stamp agreement on that clock, in a hand-started sim on the ground with no mission running: 180 poses, median 4.0 ms, 95th percentile 5.3 ms, maximum 6.6 ms. But the sim ran at little over half speed in flight. `sim/clock_bridge.py` now passes one message per 4 ms of sim time: MAVROS at 87% of a core while waiting for READY, real-time factor 0.86 to 0.88 in flight.

**3. With the stale-data guard on, the 2026 delivery cannot fly in the sim yet.** The first flight had `time_health_guard` on. It held all the way and was stopped by hand: over 500 lines of

```
[package_delivery] time base UNHEALTHY (pose is 145 ms old (limit 100 ms)): treating it as no target, holding, no descent
```

The sim delivers poses at 4.2 Hz in sim time (26 poses over 6.3 s, 250 ms apart) even though `simple_takeoff_service` asks for 20 Hz. The guard is right to refuse. The scenario therefore leaves the guard off until PLT-06's stream keeper holds the pose rate; the guard's behaviour is covered by unit tests through the real missions and executor. That flight also led to the guard's one second of hysteresis: it changed state on every other tick.
